Guideline 5.1.3 — Health and Health Research

The study is "Digital Habits and Student Well-Being: A Mixed-Methods Study of
Smartphone App Use, Emotion, and Physical Activity," led by Chao Liu, PhD,
Associate Professor of Psychology at Cedarville University. Participants must
confirm that they are at least 18 years old.

The attached Cedarville University Institutional Review Board notification
identifies IRB #374 and documents approval on September 3, 2026, under
expedited category 7. It states that approval is valid for 12 months from the
notification date.

The revised consent form describes:

- Nature, purpose, and duration: an observational study of smartphone use,
  mobility, well-being, stress, and physical activity, lasting 29 days with
  approximately 3.5–5.5 hours of active participation.
- Procedures, risks, and benefits: baseline/final surveys, daily cropped
  app-usage screenshots, step counts, private audio or optional video
  reflections, and background GPS collection during Days 1–28. Disclosures
  include fatigue, emotional discomfort, increased battery/data use, and
  privacy risks from precise locations and timestamps. There is no guaranteed
  direct benefit.
- Confidentiality and data handling: coded study identifiers, separate
  identifying records, encrypted transfer/storage, restricted researcher
  access, processing through the secure Azure-hosted system, limits on
  sharing, and aggregate or de-identified reporting. Raw GPS records are to
  be deleted after mobility measures are derived and verified, targeted
  within 30 days of verification; retained mobility measures exclude precise
  coordinates and individual travel maps. The form also explains raw-media
  deletion, code-key destruction, and retention of de-identified records.
- Contacts: Dr. Chao Liu, (937) 766-7881, for study questions; Cedarville
  University's IRB, IRB@cedarville.edu or (937) 766-7677, for participant-rights
  questions.
- Withdrawal: voluntary withdrawal without penalty and requests to delete
  identifiable information when feasible, with limits after de-identification
  or aggregation. In the app, Quit Study in settings stops collection; the
  investigator is available for study withdrawal and data-deletion questions.

Participant access and consent verification:

Study-specific adult consent is completed with the research team outside the
app. Before issuing an invitation, authorized research staff verify the completed
consent and record the participant code, approved consent version, consent date,
secure consent-register reference, verification time, and verifying staff ID.
Signed consent forms and the identifying code key are held separately from
coded app data.

StudyTrace build 13 adds a passwordless participant sign-in to the existing
invitation flow. Each consent-verified participant receives a personal QR/link,
valid for first activation for seven days. The app verifies it online and the
server binds it to that participant's device. A shared study password, arbitrary
participant code, or the generic onboarding consent choice cannot activate
research collection. The participant does not create a password or sign in each
day; verified access is remembered securely on that device.

Research collection is limited to 28 days from activation. Expired or revoked
credentials are rejected by the server across its study-data routes. The app
checks the saved deadline, refreshes status when foregrounded and periodically
while running, and stops collection when access expires or revocation is
received. Offline cached access cannot extend the original collection deadline.
Settings > Quit Study immediately stops local collection, clears local study
data, and notifies the server, with a retry if offline. A new enrollment requires
another researcher-verified invitation.

Guideline 2.5.4 — Background Location

Background location supports this study's mobility research. The revised
consent expressly describes collecting precise coordinates, timestamps, and
accuracy information throughout the study's location-collection period,
including while participants use other apps. These data support the study's
analysis of mobility in relation to well-being and physical activity.
Participants can view their collected daily history in Dashboard > Location.

We corrected storage to save delivered fixes in the Core Location callback,
preserving their acquisition timestamps. We validated background persistence
in StudyTrace 1.0.0 build 11 on a physical iPhone 14 Pro. Build 13 retains this
callback storage fix and adds the consent-verified participant sign-in described
above. The supplied physical-device recording was made with build 11; it does
not show the new sign-in UI.
